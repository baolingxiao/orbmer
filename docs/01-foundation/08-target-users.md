## Segmentation principle

The MVP should select users whose access problem is important enough to justify cross-border cost and waiting time. `Everyone who shops online` is not a useful target segment.

## Candidate buyer segments

### Access-constrained enthusiasts

Buyers seeking distinctive collectibles, design objects, specialty products, or regional goods that are unavailable through trusted domestic channels.

**Need:** discovery, source credibility, route feasibility, and reliable coordination.  
**Risk:** price sensitivity after shipping and duties; category-specific authenticity or safety issues.

### Gift and occasion buyers

Buyers seeking origin-specific or personalized products for meaningful occasions.

**Need:** clear delivery expectations, presentation quality, and proactive exception handling.  
**Risk:** deadlines magnify carrier and customs uncertainty.

### Quality-conscious global shoppers

Buyers willing to pay for curation and service rather than navigating foreign-language stores and fragmented logistics.

**Need:** understandable comparison, cost transparency, and support.  
**Risk:** expectations may resemble premium retail even when the Platform is an agent.

### Small professional buyers

Designers, studios, collectors, or small businesses sourcing limited quantities of specialist goods.

**Need:** specification accuracy, documentation, repeat ordering, and predictable communication.  
**Risk:** commercial import, tax, warranty, and resale requirements may differ from consumer transactions.

## Candidate merchant and brand users

### Regional merchants with international demand

Merchants that can fulfill reliably but lack localized acquisition, payment, support, or cross-border operations.

### Emerging brands

Brands seeking controlled exposure in selected foreign markets without immediately operating a local storefront.

### Established brands testing a market

Brands that value curated placement, demand evidence, and managed pilot volume.

## Candidate partner users

- Purchasing partners with merchant access and reliable procurement processes.
- Warehouses or consolidators capable of documented receiving and handoff.
- Logistics providers with trackable cross-border services.
- Verification specialists for categories requiring enhanced assurance.

## MVP qualification criteria

A segment is attractive when:

- the product is meaningfully difficult to access domestically;
- the buyer can understand and accept cross-border lead time;
- average order value can support variable service cost;
- the product can be legally and reliably shipped on selected lanes;
- merchant availability and product information can be verified; and
- failure modes can be remedied without disproportionate cost.

## Users not initially served

- Buyers requiring guaranteed delivery dates where the Platform cannot control the route.
- Orders involving prohibited, highly regulated, perishable, dangerous, or operationally unsupported products.
- High-volume commercial importers requiring enterprise customs and tax services unless separately approved.
- Sellers seeking immediate, unrestricted self-service listing.
- Buyers unwilling to accept disclosed import responsibilities or cross-border return limitations.

## Priority decision

> [DECISION REQUIRED: DR-009] Choose one primary buyer segment, one category wedge, source and destination countries, and explicit exclusion criteria for the pilot.

## Research required before approval

- Interview candidate buyers about recent failed or avoided cross-border purchases.
- Test willingness to pay using complete estimated landed cost, not product price alone.
- Validate merchant fulfillment capacity and data quality.
- Map category and route restrictions.
- Run concierge orders and document every handoff and exception.

> [LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED: LR-007] Determine whether consumer, commercial-buyer, age, accessibility, privacy, and marketing obligations differ across the chosen target segments and jurisdictions.
