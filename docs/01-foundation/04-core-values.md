## 1. Clarity before conversion

Explain material costs, roles, restrictions, uncertainty, and remedies before asking the buyer to commit. A higher conversion rate created by ambiguity is not a product success.

## 2. Curate with evidence

Selection must have a reason: demand signal, merchant reliability, product distinction, route feasibility, or a documented quality standard. Curation is an operating discipline, not a visual label.

## 3. One accountable experience

The Platform should provide a clear support entry point and coordinate resolution, even when the underlying merchant, warehouse, carrier, customs broker, or payment provider owns part of the process. Coordination does not mean falsely claiming total control.

## 4. Responsibility follows control

The party that controls a decision or failure point should generally own the associated operational response, subject to law and contract. The Platform should not sell assurances that its systems and partners cannot support.

## 5. Earn trust transaction by transaction

Trust should be measured through successful orders, accurate expectations, issue resolution, and repeat use—not through broad marketing claims.

## 6. Operate narrowly, learn deeply

Constrained routes and categories are preferable to shallow global coverage. Manual work is acceptable in early stages when it produces reliable learning and auditable processes.

## 7. Sustainable value for every participant

Buyer value, merchant economics, partner capacity, and Platform contribution margin must be evaluated together. Growth that depends on hidden subsidies or uncompensated partner risk is not durable.

## Decision use

When values conflict, apply this order:

1. legal and safety obligations;
2. truthful role and cost disclosure;
3. prevention of buyer or partner harm;
4. reliable transaction completion;
5. sustainable economics;
6. speed and growth.

This ordering is a product principle and does not replace jurisdiction-specific legal obligations.
