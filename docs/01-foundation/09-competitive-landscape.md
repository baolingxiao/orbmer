## Purpose

This document compares solution categories, not legal terms or copied product policies. Named platforms require current primary-source research before claims are used externally.

## Customer alternatives

| Alternative | Strength | Typical gap the Platform may address |
|---|---|---|
| Buy directly from an overseas merchant | Direct merchant relationship and potentially lower service cost | Language, payment acceptance, shipping coverage, fragmented tracking, and limited support across borders |
| Open marketplace | Large assortment, familiar search, and price competition | Variable seller quality, overwhelming choice, inconsistent cross-border service |
| Proxy purchasing service | Access to merchants that do not serve the destination | Procurement delay, complex fees, fragmented catalog, and limited post-purchase assurance |
| Package forwarding service | Gives buyers a local receiving address and shipping choice | Buyer must choose merchant and product, manage merchant disputes, and understand import risk |
| Curated retailer or boutique | Strong merchandising and unified retail experience | Narrow selection, higher inventory/markup, and limited geographic or category reach |
| Brand's local distributor | Authorized supply, local warranty, and easier returns | Limited assortment, slower product availability, or no distribution in smaller markets |
| Social/community sourcing | Discovery and human recommendations | Informal payment, inconsistent verification, poor tracking, and limited recourse |
| Personal travel or personal shopper | Flexible access and context | Low scalability, high cost, and inconsistent compliance or protection |

## Proposed differentiation hypotheses

### Curation tied to operability

Products should be selected not only for appeal but also for merchant reliability, route feasibility, information quality, and supportability.

### One visible cross-border journey

The buyer should see coherent order milestones and one support entry point across procurement, merchant, warehouse, carrier, and customs events.

### Explicit role and cost clarity

The Platform should explain who sells, who procures, who imports, which amounts are estimates, and what protection applies.

### Tiered trust

Standard products and curated/certified products may carry different verification and remedy levels, provided those levels are defined and supportable.

### Controlled expansion

The Platform should add sellers, categories, and lanes only when performance data supports the expansion.

These are hypotheses, not established competitive advantages.

## Competitive risks

- Large platforms can add curation, managed logistics, or cross-border programs.
- Proxy and forwarding services may have stronger route density and lower shipping rates.
- Direct merchants may improve international checkout and eliminate the need for an intermediary.
- A narrow catalog may fail to generate repeat demand.
- Transparent fees may expose an unattractive landed cost.
- Premium presentation may create expectations inconsistent with an agency model.

## Defensibility candidates

- reliable performance data by merchant, product, and cross-border lane;
- partner relationships and negotiated service levels;
- structured exception-resolution playbooks;
- trusted category-specific verification;
- repeat-buyer preference and demand data;
- coherent catalog context and localization; and
- operational integrations that reduce manual coordination cost.
